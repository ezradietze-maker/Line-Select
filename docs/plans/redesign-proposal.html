Why it looks AI-made today

I measured the code and the live site. The look comes from a handful of habits that most AI-built sites share. Fixing these is what makes the new design feel made by a person.

1 palette

Charcoal with a glowing amber accent. This is one of the most common dark looks on generated sites right now. It also makes every page feel the same.

86

Small, wide-spaced capital labels like "INDEPENDENT PROTOTYPE" and the numbered "01 · LOAD" markers. They decorate rather than inform.

303

Uses of the typewriter-style font. It is meant for times and numbers but appears on labels and headings too, which reads as a "techy" costume.

55

Separate CSS animations, plus 25 files using the motion library. Many are novelty touches (paper plane, turbulence shake, seatbelt sign, twinkling stars, sun and moon). Together they feel busy and unplanned.

253

Hand-set pixel text sizes, some as small as 8px. Small grey text on dark grey is the hardest thing on the site to read in a crew room or a cockpit.

Glass

Every box is the same glowing glass card with a hairline edge, so nothing stands out as more important. The 3D globe on the front page adds a heavy library to the first load without saying anything about bidding.

The good news: the site already routes almost all colour through shared settings (2,211 uses against 16 hard-coded colours), so a full re-skin is practical without touching the wording or the logic.

    A · Strip Bay Recommended

    Air traffic controllers keep each flight on a paper strip in a coloured holder, racked in order and moved by hand. Ranking bid lines is the same job. In this theme every line is a strip: a holder colour on the left shows its tier, ruled fields keep rank, line, score and key facts lined up down the page, and sorting or dragging moves the strips like re-racking a board.

    Feels like
    Tactile, organised, calm. Light console green-grey in daylight, dark console at night.
    Shapes
    Near-square corners, ruled field dividers, a real paper shadow under each strip. No glow.
    Accent
    Strip-holder yellow, used only as a fill (holders, the active page, the top pick), never as glowing text.
    Signature
    Strips slide into their slot and re-rack when you sort.
    Best for
    Dense pages: rankings, View/Add, open time, trade board. It handles the most data per screen of the three.
    Watch out
    Strip bays are controller furniture. Pilots know them but don't use them daily.

    C · Ramp at Night

    The Memphis hub at 2 a.m.: a deep navy sky and airfield lighting with fixed meanings. Blue edge lights mark boundaries, green centreline lights mark the path, yellow-on-black signs say where you are and black-on-yellow signs say where to go. In this theme the site uses those same rules. The page you're on is a location sign, main buttons are direction signs, progress is a green centreline, and red is kept for stop.

    How the themes work

    One look, three modes

    Whichever theme you choose becomes the site's identity. Pilots then pick Day, Night or Red-light from the Appearance menu, or leave it on System to follow their phone. The menu shows a small live preview of each mode.

    Switching

    The new mode sweeps across from the switch in under half a second (try the Mode buttons above). The saved choice is applied before the page draws, so there's never a flash of the wrong colours. With reduced motion turned on, the switch is instant.

    Calendar colours keep their meaning

    Flight, deadhead, layover and report time stay the colours the ex-pilot chose in every mode, adjusted only for brightness on each background. In red-light everything is red, so deadheads become dashed and layovers hatched to stay readable.

    Built on the existing switches

    The site already stores the mode on the page and swaps shared colour settings. The redesign changes those settings' values, the fonts and the shapes. Pilots' saved mode and personal calendar colour carry over unchanged.

    Readable first

    No text below 11px. Every text colour is checked against its background (4.5 to 1 or better). The site declares its colours to the browser so Safari and phones stop guessing at a dark version.

    Phone and browser chrome

    The phone's address bar, the home-screen icon and the link preview all follow the new theme and mode.

    Animations

    Today's 55 separate effects become five motions that every page shares. Each one shows something real, like a line moving to its new rank, instead of decorating. Every motion respects the phone's reduced-motion setting.
