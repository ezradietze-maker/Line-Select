# Line Select tutorial video: script

Approved Nov 15 2026. The narration and what's on screen for each chapter; the recording scripts in
`scripts/tutorial/` follow it shot by shot, and the voice is made from the quoted paragraphs.

**What's in.** Every finished feature, in the order a pilot meets them.

**What's left out:**
- View/Add and Open Time.
- The FedEx connection, which is still "coming soon".
- The old Confirm Preferences page. Nothing links to it any more; the interview has its own review screen.
- Membership and pricing. Everything is free while in testing, and the two pages don't agree yet.

**Length.** 16 chapters, about 21 minutes in all. Each chapter stands on its own, so a pilot can jump straight to one.

**Voice.** One pilot talking to another: plain words, the pack's own terms, no app jargon. About 150 words a minute.

---

## 1. What Line Select does (about 1 min)

**On screen.** The home page:
- the approach-lights animation;
- the four "How it flies" steps scrolling past (Load, Brief, Rank, Check);
- a cut to a finished rankings list.

**Narration.**
> Line Select reads your whole bid pack, asks you how you like to fly, and ranks every line in it against you — with
> the reason for each score, so you don't have to re-check it by hand.
>
> It works in four steps. Load your bid pack. Tell it what matters, the way you'd tell another pilot. Get every line
> ranked, best match first. Then check the month, and send your order over to VIPS, where you look it over and submit
> it yourself.
>
> A couple of things up front. Line Select is independent — it isn't made or endorsed by FedEx, and it never submits
> anything for you. And the score is how well a line fits you, not a guess at what you'll be awarded. You still check
> your own pack.
>
> Each chapter of this video covers one part of the site. Skip to whatever you need.

## 2. Signing in and loading your bid pack (about 1.5 min)

**On screen.**
- **Sign in:** the "Create account" tab with a test account and the terms box ticked, the recovery-code panel, then
  "Continue to Line Select".
- **Upload:** the "Load your bid pack" screen, then a PDF dragged onto the drop zone. The loader counts pages, pairings
  and lines.
- **Check it:** "Does this look right?", with the seat picker if the PDF holds both seats, and Lines, Avg credit and Avg
  days off ringed in turn. Then "Looks right — continue".
- **Try it first:** the home page's "Try it with sample data", tapped as a guest, and the sample pack loaded.

**Narration.**
> You can use Line Select as a guest, but signing in lets your pack, your interview and your rankings follow you to
> any device. Create an account with your email and a password, and agree to the terms. You'll get a recovery code
> once — save it somewhere, because it gets you back in if you ever forget your password.
>
> Then load your bid pack: the same PDF you download for your domicile, aircraft and month. Drop it on the page or
> choose the file. Line Select reads the pairings and the line grids straight from it — nothing is typed in by hand —
> and it never keeps other pilots' names or employee numbers.
>
> It shows you what it found: how many lines, the credit, the days off. If the PDF has both captain and first officer
> lines, pick your seat. If it all looks right, continue.
>
> Just want a look first? "Try it with sample data" loads a small made-up pack, clearly marked as the sample, so you
> can try everything before your own pack is out.

## 3. The interview (about 2.5 min)

**On screen.**
- "You haven't set your preferences yet", then "Start the interview".
- Seniority number, then the "Your story" box with the mic. The topic chips tick as it's typed, and the depth meter
  fills.
- The commute step, then the layover city cards, rated Avoid to Love.
- One AI question with real lines shown as examples.
- A line pair, "Which would you bid higher?", then a trip pair, with option A tapped open to the whole trip.
- Trip grading ("Love it … Trade it away").
- The review screen "Here's what we heard. Is it right?", then "Looks right — rank my lines" and the wheels-down
  landing.

**Narration.**
> The interview is how Line Select learns what you actually want. Plan on twenty to thirty minutes the first time —
> after that, a new month is a quick check-in.
>
> Start with your seniority number, if your pack has the list. It's only used to estimate what you could hold.
>
> Then the big one: walk it through how you bid, start to finish, in your own words. Type it, or tap the mic and just
> talk. The more you say, the better your ranking — the chips underneath tick off what you've covered.
>
> Tell it whether you commute, and from where. Then rate the layover cities in your pack, from Avoid to Love.
>
> After that it asks questions built from your own story. Some are in your words; some show you two real lines or two
> real trips from your pack — tap one to open it in full — and ask which you'd rather have. Near the end you grade a handful of trips, the
> way you'd size them up on a trade board. Skip anything you don't want to answer.
>
> Last, it reads it all back: your priorities, in order, with what you said next to each one. Mark anything that's off,
> then rank your lines.

## 4. Quick start and your preferences page (about 1.5 min)

**On screen.**
- **Quick start:** "Short on time? Quick start: about a minute" opens "What matters most to you this month?". Six
  priorities are tapped: Short trips, Layovers worth having, Little standby, Easy to commute, Protect my sleep, Fewer legs
  a day. "Protect my sleep" is dragged to the top and "Easy to commute" moved up one with its arrow. The "Start from a
  pilot like you" choices are pointed out. Then "Rank my lines".
- **Preferences:** "Your priorities", where one priority is changed from High to Top and another set to "Doesn't
  matter".
- **Tell us something new:** text is typed into the box, then "Add it".
- **New month:** the "New bid pack loaded" panel with "Something's changed — quick check-in".

**Narration.**
> No time for the full interview? Quick start takes about a minute. Tap the things that matter to you — here, short
> trips, layovers worth having, little standby, easy to commute, protect my sleep and fewer legs a day. Then put them
> in order: drag one above another, or use the arrows, so the one that matters most is at the top. Or start from a
> pilot like you. You can always sharpen it with the full interview later.
>
> Everything you've told Line Select is kept on your Preferences page. Each priority shows how much it counts and what
> you said that put it there. Make one count more or less, or take it off with "Doesn't matter", and your rankings
> update right away.
>
> Got a new commute, or a date you need off? Tell it in your own words under "Tell us something new", and
> it reads it into your priorities.
>
> When next month's pack comes out, load it. Your preferences carry forward, and a short check-in catches anything
> that's different.

## 5. Reading your rankings (about 2 min)

**On screen.**
- **The list:** "Your ranked lines", panning down the strips. The #1 line's score is shown, then its yellow "Top pick"
  sign.
- **The views:** a toggle between Timeline and Bid pack, and a pause on the strip key.
- **Open a line:** one line is tapped. The card shows its trips, its features chips, and the month calendar with a day
  tapped open.
- **Stars and hiding:** the star going to the shortlist, and the ✕ hiding a line further down.
- **The tour:** a glimpse of the first-visit tour ("Your bid at a glance").

**Narration.**
> Here's your month, every line ranked against you, best match first. The number on each line is how well it fits what
> you told us, out of a hundred: the higher, the better the fit. Your top pick is the yellow sign at the top.
>
> Each line reads like the pack's own page. "Bid pack" view shows it the way the pack prints it — flight numbers,
> deadheads, layover cities, day by day. "Timeline" lays each trip out from report to release, so you can see how the
> days really fall.
>
> Tap any line to open it. You get its trips, the features that stand out for you, and the whole month on a calendar.
> Tap a day for everything on it — report time, legs, layover, hotel.
>
> Star a line to keep it on your shortlist. Tap the X to hide one you'd never bid.

## 6. Inside a line: trips, rest and the body clock (about 2 min)

**On screen.**
- **Calendar tab:** "Trip details", then the Calendar tab. One trip's readouts (report, TAFB, block, duty periods), the
  hour-by-hour chart with a day opened, and "Trip score for you".
- **Rest plan:** the "Rest plan" with its sleep ribbon.
- **Body clock:** the "Body clock 64/100" badge, tapped for its reasons.
- **Score Breakdown tab:** the category bars, then "What's working" and "What's not".
- **Route tab:** the map of arcs.

**Narration.**
> Open "Trip details" for the full picture of every trip on the line. The calendar tab goes trip by trip — report,
> time away from base, block, duty periods — and an hour-by-hour chart you can tap for the pairing's own details,
> hotel and pickup times included.
>
> Each trip gets its own score for you, so you can see which trip is carrying a line and which one's dragging it down.
>
> The rest plan suggests when to sleep on each layover, worked out from when you'll get to the hotel and what your
> body clock will be doing.
>
> The body-clock score runs from 1 to 100 — the higher, the easier on you. Tap it to see exactly what costs points on
> that trip, and why.
>
> The score breakdown shows why the line scored what it did — what's working for you and what isn't — and the route
> tab maps where the line takes you.

## 7. Finding the lines you want (about 2 min)

**On screen.**
- **Ask your pack:** "Lines with Paris and at least 14 days off" is typed, and the answer list appears.
- **Sort:** "Sort: Best match" opens the sort list. A search for "body clock", the preview pane, then "Sort by this".
  Then "Back to your ranking".
- **Filters:** "Filters" opens. Days off 15+ and "No night legs" are set, then "Only lines I can commute to and from" is
  switched on, holding on what the real-flight check found.
- **Compare:** two lines are picked with "Compare", and the "Compare lines" dialog opens.
- **Jump:** ⌘K, typing a line number.

**Narration.**
> Looking for something specific? Ask your pack in plain words — "lines with Paris and at least 14 days off" — and it
> hands back the pack's own lines that match, in your order, with the figures behind each one.
>
> Sort by almost anything: credit, days off, weekends, hub turns, the body-clock score — around ninety ways. Sorting is
> only for looking; your bid still follows your ranking.
>
> Filters narrow the list: days off, credit, report times, deadheads, layover cities. If you commute, it can keep only
> the lines you can actually commute to and from on real airline flights.
>
> To weigh two lines against each other, press "Compare" on each. You get them side by side — days off, credit, body
> clock, and both months on the calendar.
>
> And if you know the line number, press Command-K — Control-K on Windows — and type it to jump straight there.

## 8. Putting your order right: swaps and tiers (about 2 min)

**On screen.**
- **Swap:** a line is dragged by its handle onto another, and they swap. The banner reads "Swapped … — they'll stay
  that way", with what it learned.
- **The why question:** "Why does line X beat Y for you?", where a chip is picked.
- **Tiers:** a line is opened and the "Best" tier tapped. Then 1–5 on the keyboard. A shift-click selects several, and
  the right-click menu moves them to a tier.
- **Set aside and undo:** "Set aside", then the Undo button.

**Narration.**
> Line Select's order is a starting point — you have the final say. If a line is in the wrong place, drag it by its
> handle onto another line and the two swap. They stay where you put them.
>
> Every swap also teaches it something, and it tells you what it took from it. If it can't tell why you swapped, it
> asks you, once: tap the reason, or say they're about equal.
>
> Tiers let you group your lines: Best, Better, Good, Backup and Not bid. Open a line and tap a tier, or press a number
> key. To move a whole run at once, shift-click to select them, or right-click for "select all above" or "below", and
> move them together. Anything in "Not bid" stays out of your bid.
>
> "Set aside" takes a line out of the way while you sort, without dropping it from your bid. Change your mind on
> anything? Undo.

## 9. This month's bid: deadline, carry-in, vacation and your chances (about 2 min)

**On screen.**
- **Deadline:** the "Bid closes" countdown, with "Remind me in my calendar".
- **Checklist:** the four items, shown but not ticked: nothing has been bid yet.
- **Carry-in:** "Carry-in from last month" opens and a made-up trip (Sep 30 to Oct 3) is added. The bid-question
  checkboxes are shown, then the closed panel says how many top lines lose a trip.
- **Vacation:** "Your vacation" with Use it / Keep my flying, on a pack with a vacation schedule.
- **Your chances:** the drawer opens. Then the "Realistic" view and a forecast chip on a line ("Likely · 64% open").

**Narration.**
> At the top of your rankings is this month's bid at a glance: how long until bidding closes, and a checklist of where
> you are. Set a reminder in your calendar so you don't miss the close.
>
> If a trip from last month runs into this one, add it under carry-in. Your lines are then scored as they'll really fly,
> with any trip it knocks off taken into account.
>
> If the pack's vacation schedule shows you have vacation this month, choose how to treat it: use it, or keep your
> flying and let the lines it barely touches rise.
>
> And with your seniority number in, "Your chances" estimates which lines could still be open when it's your turn.
> "Realistic" shows only the lines you have a real shot at — and every line shows its odds.

## 10. Sending your bid to VIPS (about 2 min)

**On screen.**
- "Ready to bid?", where "Copy line order" changes to "Copied — cleared to bid".
- **The send dialog:** "Send to VIPS" opens. The numbered choices, then "If none of these are left" with the
  catch-alls ticked, then the bid questions with "What should I answer for this order?".
- **The checks:** "Before you send", with one warning and its "Use these" fix.
- **Send:** "Open VIPS and send", then "VIPS opened in a new tab".
- **Other ways out:** a quick look at "Copy with notes", "Add top pick to calendar" and "Print".

**Narration.**
> When your order looks right, it's time to bid. "Copy line order" copies your whole ranked order, one line per row,
> ready to enter yourself. It always follows your ranking and tiers — not whatever sort you happen to be looking at —
> and leaves out anything you've marked Not bid.
>
> Or use "Send to VIPS". Choose what happens if none of your lines are left — any regular line, secondary, or reserve.
> Answer VIPS's bid questions; nothing is picked for you, but it can tell you what it would answer for this order and
> why.
>
> Before you send, it checks your bid — for example, whether you're likely to run out of lines at your seniority — and
> where it has a fix, one tap puts it in.
>
> Then "Open VIPS and send" opens FedEx's own site with your order filled in. You sign in there — Line Select never
> sees your password, and nothing is sent until you submit it yourself.

## 11. Strategies (about 1.5 min)

**On screen.**
- **Your place:** "Your strategies", with the standing bar showing the little plane taxiing to your place.
- **A strategy:** one strategy card, with "Your move" and its recommended lines ("Likely open").
- **Vacation slide:** the slide finder. The vacation slider is dragged across the calendar, and suggestion chips
  appear. *(Re-record: Nov 19 the slide finder left Strategies. The vacation is on every line of My Rankings now,
  with a "What do you want from your vacation?" box at the top; chapter 9 should show it, and this beat comes out.)*
- **What pilots held:** the "What pilots near you actually held" panel.

**Narration.**
> Strategies turns your pack's own numbers into real bidding moves for where your seniority lands this month. It starts
> with your real place — not just your number on the list, but where you stand once you count who's above you and not
> bidding a line.
>
> Then it lays out the moves that apply to you, with the steps and the lines to bid for each, and whether each line is
> likely to still be open.
>
> The vacation slide finder tries every legal slide of your vacation on your top lines and shows which one adds the
> most days off or banks the most pay. Drag it along the calendar to see it for yourself.
>
> And as pilots report what they held, you'll see what pilots near your seniority actually got.
>
> These are worked out from the pack, not the official rules — check anything you're unsure of with your bid rep.

## 12. Cities and hotels (about 1.5 min)

**On screen.**
- **Cities:** "Layover cities", where Los Angeles is tapped. The pop-up shows local time, sunrise and sunset, the
  weather, the hotel, the stays in this pack, and "Your take" (the demo pilot loves LAX).
- **Hotel Ratings:** one hotel card with its rating gauge. "What's nearby & what reviewers say" opens to the amenity
  tiles and the review themes.

**Narration.**
> Cities shows every layover city in your pack: the local time, daylight, the weather, and how often the pack sends
> you there. Tap one for the whole picture — typical weather for these dates, the hotel, and every stay in this pack,
> with links to the lines that have them. Your own rating is there too, and you can change it in Preferences.
>
> Hotel Ratings goes a step further. It shows the actual hotel the pack assigns each layover, rated from Google, with
> what's within walking distance — food, a gym, a pharmacy, coffee — and what recent reviews say about noise, the beds
> and the area.

## 13. Logbook and your calendar (about 1.5 min)

**On screen.**
- **Log an award:** "Log your … award", with "A regular line" and the line picked. The share checkbox and its note,
  then "Log it".
- **How it flew:** "How did … fly?", with "Better than I expected".
- **Over time:** the stats and the "Where you've laid over" chips.
- **Calendar link:** "Subscribe in your calendar", then "Make my link". The Apple, Google and Outlook buttons appear;
  the private link itself is blurred.
- **Date.** This chapter, and the next one's Pay check, run on Oct 27, with the award out and the month nearly flown.

**Narration.**
> Once the award is out, log it in your logbook — which line you got. After you've flown the month, tell it how it
> went. Over the months it adds up to what you've held, where you've been, and where your seniority's heading — and it
> helps your next ranking.
>
> You can choose to share your award with other pilots. It shows only your seniority number, never your name or
> employee number, and you can withdraw it any time.
> Shared awards are what make "Your chances" and "what pilots held" more accurate for everyone.
>
> Signed in with your own pack, you can also subscribe in your calendar. Make your link once and add it to Apple,
> Google or Outlook. The trips on your top line show up on their own and stay up to date, and once you log your
> award, the calendar shows that instead.

- **Commute planner:** one line picked, with the real flights listed for a trip.
- **Pay check:** "Bid Line Guarantee", then the "Each trip's guarantee" table. Under "Paid less than a trip guaranteed?", an

**Narration.**
> Tools has a world clock for everywhere your pack goes, with a map of who's in daylight, and a converter for "what time

>
> If you commute, the commute planner lists real airline flights to each trip's report and back home after it, for

>
> Pay check shows what each trip and your whole line guarantee this bid period — the floors you're paid to however the
> month actually flies — with the contract section each one comes from. If a trip got rebuilt or cancelled and you were
> paid less than it guaranteed, enter what you were paid, and it drafts a pay claim for you to check and file yourself.

## 16. Trade Board, and getting around (about 1.5 min)

**On screen.**
- **Trade Board:** the sample pack's example offer ("Example — not real"), then "Post a trade offer".
- **Trades found for you:** the swap card ("You give" / "You get", "+X on your score"), on a mock-up if the seat isn't
  open.
- **Inbox:** the Inbox, then the Trade Board in the menu.
- **Getting around:** the left menu's three groups, the Appearance menu switched to Light, then Night (red cockpit
  lighting), then back to Dark, "What's new", and "Send feedback".

**Narration.**
> The Trade Board is where pilots in your seat post trips they'd trade and answer each other's offers — this one's a
> clearly marked example. Nothing here is a real trade: it's for finding each other, and real trades still go through
> FedEx's own process after bidding closes. Once you confirm what you hold, Line Select can also suggest trades that
> leave everyone on them better off.
>
> Your Inbox collects anything waiting on you. The board opens for your domicile, aircraft and seat once enough pilots
> there are using Line Select.
>
> A few last things. The Appearance menu switches between light and dark, and Night mode turns everything red to
> protect your night vision. "What's new" shows what's changed lately. And if anything's broken or confusing, "Send
> feedback" goes straight to the person building it.
>
> That's Line Select. Load your pack, tell it how you fly, and bid with confidence.

## Recording notes

- **Pack.** Recorded on the real October 2026 Memphis 767 pack (first officer), which the owner approved. Parsed line
  data carries no names. The sample pack is used only for quick start's guest and the Trade Board's example offer.
- **Dates.** The browser's clock is set to two days before October's bid closes, so the countdown runs and Send to
  VIPS shows no "bid closed" stop. Nothing is ever sent to FedEx: "Open VIPS and send" is only pointed at.
- **Shown, not working:** "Held by seniority" and the "what pilots held" numbers need 5 pilots sharing, so they're
  narrated but only glimpsed. Trade matching on a real seat needs 15 pilots, so the board is shown with the sample
  pack's example offer.
- **Account.** A demo account on a throwaway local database. No real names, employee numbers or keys on screen.
